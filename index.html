Centline

For a one-truck owner-operator deciding, at a loading dock at night, whether a load is worth taking.

A broker quotes dollars per loaded mile. The empty miles you drive to reach the pickup cost exactly the same and pay nothing, which is the usual reason a new carrier goes under. Centline takes the truck’s real costs once, works out a true cost per mile, and answers what a load nets after deadhead, then builds the detention clock, the invoice, and the broker payment record on that same number. No backend, no accounts, no network calls, no analytics: it all runs on the device.

About

I study informatics at Iowa, specifically human-computer interaction, with minors in Spanish and psychology. That combination is not incidental.

Almost everything I have built is for someone operating inside a language or a system that was not designed with them in mind: a resident reading a government page written for citizens, a driver quoted a rate that hides the empty miles, a diner holding a menu they cannot read. The design half of my training is what makes me ask who is on the other side of the screen. The engineering half is what lets me ship the answer.

I work on both sides of the handoff. I take a Figma file and ship it, and I argue with it when the spec and the device cannot both be right. The habit I would most like to be judged on is the one in the log above: I measure before I claim, I write down the reasoning that is not obvious so the next person does not have to rediscover it, and when I am wrong I go and check rather than defend it.

In practice that meant Flutter and FastAPI for Sollo in Tokyo this past summer. I am back in Iowa City now, writing SQL for the Department of Internal Medicine at University of Iowa Health Care and finishing my degree. I am looking for UI/UX design and software engineering work: full-stack, mobile-leaning, and close to the people using it.
