Internship, Tokyo, June to August 2026

Sollo

Eight weeks in Tokyo as Sollo’s UI/UX engineering intern. I designed and built an app that helps foreign residents of Japan get through government paperwork, and it shipped to the App Store.

What Sollo is

Sollo is a company in Tokyo. Its app helps foreign residents of Japan find official help: visas and residency, benefits, healthcare, housing and local services. Its assistant, Ask Sollo, answers from real government documents, in plain language and in nine languages.

The app, Sollo Life Nav, has been on the App Store since 18 August 2026.

Why I was there

Working in Japan was a goal I set before the school year started, and I followed through. I applied through CIEE, and the interview pushed hard on what I knew. I liked that. Sollo gave me real UI/UX ownership, let me go all out on the designs, and then asked me to defend them. Defending my work is what taught me the most.

For those eight weeks I was a foreigner in Tokyo too, so I was designing for a situation I was living every day.

What was mine

I designed and I built. When a new feature came in, I redrew the page, showed the team options side by side, and built the one they chose. Seeing the options together lets a team decide faster and with fewer surprises.

The design system, its light and dark theme, and every new string in nine languages were mine.

01

What I did

  • 11navigation designs, in 3 rounds

    Redesigned the navigation

    I drew the app’s navigation eleven ways over three rounds, rebuilt the signed-in app around the one we picked, and connected it to the live services. Comparing many options early is cheaper than rebuilding the wrong one later.

  • 9languages, every new string

    Shipped every string in nine languages

    Nothing went out in English with a fallback, Easy Japanese included. The app exists for people who don’t read official Japanese, so an English-only screen would have shut out the people it was built for.

  • 8×faster, and 3.1 MB lighter

    Made the app faster on purpose

    A thirty pull request performance sprint cut 3.1 MB from the app bundle, the code a phone downloads, and made the core browse flow go from 1,033ms to 125ms. People dealing with paperwork are already stressed; a slow app reads as a broken one.

  • 2.72 → 4.60contrast ratio, now passing

    Caught accessibility misses before readers did

    Contrast ratio measures how readable text is against its background. A colour in a design handoff measured 2.72:1, below the WCAG AA minimum, so I shipped it at 4.60:1, one step up the designer’s own palette so it still looked like their design. A tap target, the area a finger can press, was specced at 36 points and shipped at 48.

  • 1design system, light and dark

    Built the design system

    Colour, spacing and type tokens (named values every screen shares), a shared widget and icon library, and a light and dark theme, all checked automatically against WCAG AA contrast. An automated check keeps contrast from slipping when a deadline is close.

  • 3layers: design, app, backend

    Worked across the whole stack, feedback included

    I worked in design, the mobile app and the Python backend, and shipped in-app feedback and bug reporting end to end, from the button a reader taps to the report the team reads. A design only counts once it works for real, and owning every layer meant I could get it there.

The call I would make again

Sixteen days after I built the five-tab navigation, I cut it down to a single screen for the public beta. Three of the five tabs were still “coming soon”, and tabs that open onto nothing aren’t navigation. I parked the dock instead of deleting it, so it’s ready when those parts ship.

04

How I thought it through

  1. The core screen took a full second to open

    What I saw
    Browsing guides, the thing people do most in the app, took 1,033ms to respond.
    What I decided
    Run a focused performance sprint, thirty pull requests, instead of patching one slow spot.
    Why
    People open this app when they’re already stressed about paperwork. A delay that long reads as broken.
    Result
    1,033ms125msEight times faster, and 3.1 MB less for every phone to download.

  2. A colour in the design was too faint to read

    What I saw
    A colour in a design handoff measured 2.72:1 against its background, below the WCAG AA minimum for readable text.
    What I decided
    Move it one step up the designer’s own palette instead of picking a new colour.
    Why
    It had to pass for readers with low vision and still look like the designer’s work.
    Result
    2.72:14.60:1Passes AA, and 7.75:1 in dark mode.

  3. The way out of a failed search was too small

    What I saw
    The button that gets you out of a search with no results was specced at 36 points.
    What I decided
    Ship it at 48 points, the larger tap target, the area a finger can press.
    Why
    That’s the button you need most when you’re stuck, so it should be the easiest to hit.
    Result
    36 points48 pointsEasier to hit one handed, on the screen where people are most frustrated.

  4. Three of five tabs led nowhere

    What I saw
    Sixteen days after I built the five-tab navigation, three tabs were still “coming soon”.
    What I decided
    Cut it to one surface for the public beta and park the dock for later.
    Why
    Tabs that open onto nothing teach people the app is unfinished. Fewer, working doors beat more, empty ones.
    Result
    Five tabsOne surfaceShipped to the App Store on 18 August 2026, with the dock ready when the rest ships.
