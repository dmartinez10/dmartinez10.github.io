S1Internship, Tokyo, June to August 2026

Sollo

Eight weeks in Tokyo as Sollo’s UI/UX engineering intern, designing and building an app that helps foreign residents of Japan get through government paperwork. It is on the App Store now.

What Sollo is

Sollo is a company in Tokyo. Its app helps foreign residents of Japan find official help: visas and residency, benefits, healthcare, housing and local services. Its assistant, Ask Sollo, answers from real government documents, in plain language and in nine languages.

The app, Sollo Life Nav, has been on the App Store since 18 August 2026.

Why I was there

Japan was the dream first. Before the school year started I told my parents that the next summer I would be interning in Japan, and then I went and chased it. Tech is what I have always been into, and Japan is where I wanted to see it made.

I applied through CIEE, and I still remember the interview: they kept pushing me and testing what I knew, and I loved it. Sollo handed me the UI/UX work I had never had before, believed in me, let me go all out on the designs, and then made me defend them. Defending them taught me the most.

For those eight weeks I was a foreigner in Tokyo too, so the reader was never hard to picture.

What was mine

I designed and I built. A design existed when I arrived, and from there every new feature or idea meant I redrew the page myself, showed the team the options, and built the one they agreed on. Plenty of pages got built more than once.

The design system and its light and dark theme were mine, and so was every new string in nine languages.

  • 70+pull requests merged in eight weeks

What I did

  • Redesigned the navigation. I drew it eleven ways in three turns, rebuilt the signed-in app to the pick, and wired it to the live services.
  • Redrew the pages as the product moved. A new feature meant a new design from me, options to the team, then I built whichever one they agreed on. Several versions of the same page, until it was right.
  • Built the design system. Colour, spacing and type tokens, a shared widget and icon library, and a light and dark theme, all under an automated WCAG AA contrast check.
  • Shipped every string in nine languages. Nothing went out as English with a fallback, Easy Japanese included.
  • Caught accessibility misses before readers did. A colour in a design handoff measured 2.72:1, so it shipped at 4.60:1, one step up the designer’s own palette. A tap target the spec sized at 36 points shipped at 48.
  • Made it faster on purpose. A thirty pull request performance sprint cut 3.1 MB from the app bundle and made a card open eight times quicker.
  • Shipped feedback end to end. In-app feedback and bug reporting, from the entry point a reader taps to the report landing where the team reads it.
  • Worked across the whole stack: design, the mobile app and the Python backend.

The call I would make again

Sixteen days after I built the five-tab navigation, I cut it to a single screen for the public beta. Three of the five tabs were still “coming soon”, and a row of doors that open onto nothing is not navigation. The dock was parked, not deleted, for when those parts ship.
