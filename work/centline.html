C1Design and engineering, 2026, in testing

Centline

One record for every load, from saved to paid, for a man reading it one-handed at a loading dock at night.

Role
Sole designer and product engineer. I make the product decisions, own the design system and write the rules. Claude Code is in the loop: it draws the options I choose between and checks the work against those rules.
Constraint
A one-truck owner-operator, often in his first year, reading it one-handed at a loading dock at night. Not a software person, and short on time.
Outcome
Four tabs, 5,888 tests passing and 144 merged pull requests since 29 July 2026. In testing and not yet in the App Store, so no one uses it for real yet.
01

Cut to two jobs

Centline began as a tool for pricing loads. About six weeks in I removed the entire cost side, because the real pain is getting from delivered to paid. Nine days later the Network tab went: brokers would never offer loads through the app, so it had no job.

What is left does two things, in four tabs: moves a load from saved to delivered, and gets it invoiced and paid. Now opens on the load he is on, drawn as a green highway sign with the stage, the day, the lane, the rate and the load number, and one button that matches the moment:

02

How I work

Every change goes the same way: drawn as options, picked, written down, and only then built, checked and walked on a phone.

  1. 1Notes on the real thing

    I write notes on screenshots of the app on my phone. The Profile redraw started as seven.

  2. 2Options, side by side

    Claude Code draws the change on a canvas two to four ways, with a recommendation marked. I answer with a letter.

  3. 3The pick, written down

    Into the project’s own rules, with its date and my reason, so a later change has to argue with it.

  4. 4A spec, then a plan

    Numbered tasks before any code. 28 specs and 25 plans in eight weeks.

  5. 5Built to the plan

    Each task is built to its spec, then checked against the written rules.

  6. 6An agent that breaks it

    Its only job is to make the finished change fail.

  7. 7I look at every screen

    The app draws each screen at phone size, so I see it rather than trust it.

  8. 8I walk it

    On a simulator, then on my own phone.

  9. 9A pull request, or nothing

    Nothing reaches main without passing the required checks.

03

One reference

One reference decides the look: a parcel tracking page for a freight load. One subject in one state, the status at the top, facts in rows, the one action that matches the stage. It rules out, for free, hero imagery, streaks, badges, celebrations and any score on a safety record.

The tokens live in one design document, in an open design-token format, and one theme file in the code; nothing hard-codes a colour anywhere else. The document is linted for WCAG contrast, and the lint caught two failures I had looked at and approved. There is a night palette, because the core scene is a dock at 2am. Even a field’s outline is its own token: WCAG asks 3:1 for an input’s edge, and the old hairline measured 1.4:1.

04

Drawn before it was built

The paperwork is where a load turns into money, so it was drawn in full first.

The invoice packet is one PDF: the invoice, then its paperwork in a fixed order, handed to the phone’s share sheet. It cannot go without the signed bill of lading and the rate confirmation, and the button says why before the tap. Try it:

Send the packet

Drawn in HTML and CSS. The names are made up.

A tick means the document is in the packet, and in the app the box cannot be tapped: a checklist he could tick by hand let an invoice claim paperwork it did not carry. When the share sheet closes, the app asks whether he sent it, because only he knows. Yes starts the payment clock.

The app never claims a file needs a signature, because it cannot know. And every field is asked where he learns it: the pickup number is quoted at the gate and the BOL number is on the paper at the dock, so they wait for a section that appears once the load is accepted.

05

Rules it will not break

Some rules are held by tests rather than by review, so they cannot quietly drift.

  • Every phone. Every screen fits every phone from 280 to 440 points wide, measured at 8 phone sizes and 5 tablet windows with the real fonts. No ellipsis, a word shrinks rather than splitting, and an instruction sits under a field’s label, never inside it.
  • No long dashes in anything the app draws.
  • No grey hint text inside a field, unless I approved that one.
  • No money anywhere on the profile.
  • Nothing graded. His carrier record for brokers and the roadside inspection view never count, score, grade or colour his history red. Missing data must never read as a failing grade.
  • No run-on copy. No two lines that run together into one word.
06

Where tests were not enough

  • 11real defects in one night on the Profile redraw, from the agent whose only job is breaking things. Three were in the first pass’s own fixes.
  • 1date range reading “1 Jan to 31 Dec”, no year. 5,280 passing tests said nothing. Looking at the screen did.
  • 4defects from the first walk through a driver joining a company, which 5,759 tests could not find.

Walking it changes decisions, not only code. I walked onboarding myself, got through with a blank, and it became the one form allowed to block. The tab bar moved between four and five tabs several times, each time after using it.

07

What is not done

It is not in the App Store yet, and no one uses it for real. A long packet’s file size has never been measured on a real phone. The tablet layout has never been seen on a real tablet. The public page is at centline.co.
