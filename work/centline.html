My own app, 2026, in testing

Centline

A phone app for truck drivers who own their one truck. Every load is one record, from the day it's saved to the day it's paid. I design it and build it on my own.

What Centline is

Centline is for an owner-operator: a driver who owns one truck and runs it as his own business. Often he's in his first year, using his phone one-handed at a loading dock at night. Centline keeps each load in one place: where it stands, its paperwork, and the invoice that goes to the broker (the company that hired him for the load) with that paperwork attached.

It's in testing and not on the App Store yet, so no one uses it for real yet.

Why I built it

My dad has driven a truck for 35 years, and he owns his own. Driving is only half the job. The other half is paperwork, and the money waits on it. I built Centline for the stretch between delivering a load and getting paid for it, because that's where I saw the most time lost.

What was mine

All of it: the product decisions, the design system, the rules and the code. Claude Code, an AI coding tool, draws the options I choose from and checks the work against rules I wrote. Nothing merges into the app until I approve it, because I'm accountable for what ships.

What I did

  • 5,888automated tests

    Rules held by tests, not by memory

    Every screen has to fit every phone from 280 to 440 points wide, and nothing in the app ever grades a driver’s inspection record. Tests check both on every change. I rely on tests because a rule I remember today is one I can forget later. A test runs every time.

  • 28design specs, written before the code

    Options before code

    Each change gets drawn two to four ways. I pick one, write down why, then build it and test it on my own phone. Comparing real options side by side leads to a better decision than debating a single idea.

  • 13screen sizes checked

    One design system

    Every colour comes from one set of design tokens (named colours the whole app shares), each checked against WCAG, the accessibility standard for contrast. There's a night palette because drivers read it at a loading dock at 2am.

03

The call I would make again

Centline started as a tool for pricing loads. Six weeks in, I cut the whole cost side. It was a hard call, but the real pain wasn't pricing. It was getting from delivered to paid. What's left does two jobs well instead of five jobs halfway: move the load, and get it paid.

04

How I thought it through

  1. The app tried to do too much

    What I saw
    Centline did five jobs: pricing, deadhead (the empty miles driven to reach a load), a load check, a broker network, and invoicing. Only one of them was the real pain.
    What I decided
    Cut everything except moving the load and getting it paid, and drop a tab with it.
    Why
    Drivers lose the most time between delivering a load and getting paid. Doing two jobs well beats doing five halfway.
    Result
    5 jobs, 5 tabs2 jobs, 4 tabsNow, Loads, Invoices and Profile. Less to dig through.

  2. An invoice could go out missing its paperwork

    What I saw
    A broker won't pay without the signed bill of lading (the receipt for the delivered freight) and the rate confirmation (the agreed price). The old checklist let a driver tick boxes without attaching them.
    What I decided
    Turn the checklist into a packet: the invoice won't send until both papers are in it.
    Why
    The money waits on that paperwork. A missing page means a late payment.
    Result
    A checklist you could tickA packet that can’t lieEvery invoice goes out complete.

  3. Input fields were hard to see

    What I saw
    The edge of each input was a faint hairline at 1.4:1 contrast against the background.
    What I decided
    Darken the edge until it passes.
    Why
    WCAG asks for at least 3:1 so people can find where to type, and this app gets used in bad light.
    Result
    1.4:13:1Every field is visible at a glance.

  4. Passing tests didn’t mean nothing was broken

    What I saw
    Tests only prove what they check. A change could pass every test and still be wrong.
    What I decided
    Add a breaker: an AI agent whose only job is to make a finished change fail before it merges.
    Why
    It's cheaper to find a defect myself than to have a driver find it.
    Result
    11 defectsReal ones, found in one night, before any of them shipped.
