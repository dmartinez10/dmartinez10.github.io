Centline

A phone app for truck drivers who own their one truck. Every load is one record, from saved to paid. I design it and I build it, on my own.

What Centline is

It's for a driver who owns his one truck and runs it as his own business. A lot of the time he's in his first year, holding his phone in one hand at a loading dock at night. Centline keeps each load in one place, from the day it's saved to the day it's paid: where it stands, its paperwork, and the invoice that goes out with that paperwork attached.

It's in testing and not in the App Store yet, so nobody uses it for real yet.

Why I built it

My dad has driven a truck for 35 years, and he owns his own. Driving is only half the job. The other half is paperwork, and the money waits on it. I built Centline for that half, the stretch between delivering a load and getting paid for it, because that's where I saw the most time go.

What was mine

All of it: the product decisions, the design system, the rules and the build. Claude Code draws the options I pick from and checks the work against my rules. Nothing merges until I say so, because I'm the one who has to answer for it.

What I did

  • Every product decision, including cutting a whole half of the app when it turned out to be the wrong half.
  • The design system. Every colour comes from one set of tokens, checked for WCAG contrast. There's a night palette because he's reading it at a loading dock at 2am.
  • Rules held by tests, not by memory. Every screen fits every phone from 280 to 440 points wide, and nothing ever grades a driver’s record. I put them in tests because I'll forget a rule at some point, and a test won't.
  • Options before code. Each change gets drawn two to four ways, I pick one and write down why, then I build it and walk it on my own phone. I do it this way because it's way easier to pick between real options than to argue about one.

Try it: one button for the moment

The app opens on the load he's on, drawn as a green highway sign, with one button for where the load is right now. I went with one button because nobody with one truck opens an app to read reports. If it wastes his time, he deletes it.

03

The call I would make again

Centline started as a tool for pricing loads. Six weeks in, I cut the whole cost side. Honestly it was hard to do, but the real pain is getting from delivered to paid, not pricing. What's left does two jobs well instead of five jobs halfway: move the load, and get it paid.

04

What changed

  • Five jobs→TwoPricing, deadhead, the load check and the broker network came out. What's left moves the load and gets it paid
  • Five tabs→FourNow, Loads, Invoices, Profile. Fewer jobs meant one less tab to dig through
  • 1.4:1→3:1An input’s edge. WCAG asks for it to be visible, and the old hairline just wasn’t
  • A checklist you could tick→A packet that cannot lieThe invoice won’t send without the signed bill of lading and the rate confirmation, because the money waits on that paperwork
  • 0→5,888Tests holding the rules, including the ones a review would miss
  • Trusting the suite→Breaking it on purpose11 real defects found in one night by the agent whose only job is to make a finished change fail. Green tests only prove what they check
