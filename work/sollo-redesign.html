S1Sollo, Tokyo, summer 2026

Drawn in turns

Before I rebuilt the app, its navigation was drawn eleven ways in three turns, under rules every option had to meet. Sixteen days after the rebuild, most of it came out again, and that is the part worth telling.

Role
UI/UX engineering intern. I explored the navigation on the canvas, rebuilt the whole signed-in app to the chosen design, and took it to production.
Constraint
Four surfaces of equal weight and no home base, for foreign residents of Japan working through government paperwork, in nine languages.
Outcome
Rebuilt on 8 July as a five-tab shell, wired to live services in nine languages with dark mode, under an automated WCAG AA gate. Sixteen days later it became a single surface for the public beta.
01

Four surfaces, no home base

The signed-in app had four peer surfaces: Life Navigator, Solo Discovery, Parallel Presence and Solo Canvas. None of them was home. A stock tab bar would have switched between them and looked like every other app on the phone.

The app already carried a boarding-pass and navigator motif, so the brief was short: flip between four equal surfaces, and do it in a way that reads as owned, not generic.

02

Three turns on one canvas

I explored it on a design canvas in three turns, each a board of phones drawn side by side. Pick a turn:

Show a turn

Three switchers that carry the motif. A ticket-stub dock with a perforated edge and flight-style codes, pass tabs stamped at the top, and a floating navigator orb that fans out the other three. Same job in all three.

Three wild cards, no brief attached. A slim rail down the left edge, a dark home where the four surfaces glow like portals, and a pull-up sheet with no chrome at rest. Different paradigms, different moods, and a dark one.

Five options mixing the best of both. The canvas recommended 3a, a big labelled dock with the active tab filled solid orange, and marked 3c, plain-speech tabs at the top that name where you are, as runner-up.

Every option in turn three had to meet the same four rules:

  • Labels always visible. Never an icon on its own.
  • Large targets, 60px or more.
  • A high-contrast active state, so the current surface is never a guess.
  • Nothing hidden. You can always see where you are and where you can go.
03

One surface, twelve bars

The same canvas drew other questions at the same resolution. One was a single surface, where search is the app and the navigation lives in a ticket card at the bottom, drawn in the night palette and the day palette. Another was one component, the Your Journeys bar that leads back into a checklist in progress, drawn twelve ways.

04

Built on 8 July

On 8 July I rebuilt the whole signed-in app to the chosen design: a five-tab shell with a back stack for each tab, and the assistant’s chat wired to the live answer server so it streams sourced answers. The next day it moved onto the real services, the government guides, the reader’s journeys and the account, with every new string in nine languages and design tokens that follow the phone into dark mode.

On 10 July I put its palette under an automated WCAG AA contrast gate. It moved seven token values to pass, and flagged one exception that belonged to the brand instead of quietly retuning it.

05

Sixteen days later

For the public beta on 24 July, the five tabs became one surface. Three of the five were coming-soon placeholders, and a dock of doors that open onto nothing is not navigation. The header became the product name with a beta badge, the account became an avatar in the corner, and feedback moved one tap from home.

The visual calls went the same way as the navigation. Before writing a line, I showed the founder interactive design decks rendered on the real screen: BETA-mark variants, ten feedback entry points, twelve profile-control treatments and seven halo studies. The dock turn three recommended was parked, not deleted, for when those surfaces ship.

06

The smallest version of the habit

The same loop works at the size of one number. Asked for more air under a page title, I did not pick a value. I drew the one number four ways, 6pt as it shipped, 14pt, 20pt as the design file specified, and 28pt, plus a slider, with a note: pick one and I will set it. Try it:

‹Visa & Residency

  • Working outside your status
  • Re-entry permit
  • Residence Card
  • Permanent residency

Drawn in HTML and CSS for this page. The real board is below.

The founder picked, and I set it once, in the shared surface every page scrolls on. Auditing the result found four screens adding their own inset on top, a 20 to 32pt spread across the app, and zeroing them made every page space its content the same way. Eleven navigation options or four values for one gap: it is the same loop.
