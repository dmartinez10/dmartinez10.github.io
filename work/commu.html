U1Course project, University of Iowa, 2026

CommU

A web app that helps students who commute find out what’s happening on campus, and find each other. I ran the research, designed it and built it, front end and back end.

What CommU is

Students who commute show up for class and leave again. CommU puts campus events in one place, lets commuters find each other by the days they’re on campus, and lets any student post a meetup of their own.

I made it for a human-computer interaction course that asked for a working web app, not a prototype in a design tool. I liked that, because a prototype can’t tell you if the thing actually works.

Why this project

It was my project for Human-Computer Interaction, CS 2520. I picked commuters because most campus tools are built for students who live in the dorms, and commuters a lot of the time struggle to feel like they belong.

What was mine

Everything. I recruited and ran the five interviews, analysed them, designed the app, and built the front end and the back end. I had one semester, and the course required that it actually run.

01

What I did

  • Recruited and ran five interviews: four commuters and one student who lives on campus. I added that one on purpose, because if you only talk to people who agree with you, nobody can prove you wrong.
  • Turned them into three findings, and built nothing a finding didn’t ask for. I didn’t want to add stuff just to add it.
  • Designed and built the whole app: eight pages in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with a Flask and MongoDB backend. The course wanted it working for real, so I built both ends myself.
02

Try it: what they said

Show a finding

I usually just show up a couple minutes before class and after school I have to go to work, which is about 30 minutes away. So, I usually don’t spend much time on campus.

P2, community college, one hour commute

Time on campus is only for class. It’s not that they don’t care. There’s just no room in the day for anything else.

Sometimes I get sent an email about upcoming events, other times it’s word of mouth through friends, and posters that are around buildings.

P4, engineering, walks in

Nobody knew what was going on. So the app puts every event in one place, with search and filters.

Yes, I would love to connect with people. I love meeting new people, but I just don’t put myself out there. If there’s an easier way, then yeah.

P5, allied health, 30 minute drive

The barrier was never motivation. There was just no place to meet, so the app gives them one.

03

The answer that changed the build

I first pitched CommU as a place to find events. Then I asked one student what he would change, and he said this:

It would be to host some sort of event to meet other people, because there have been people that I’ve met that live in my same complex and I had no idea.

P4, on what he would change

He didn’t want a better feed. He wanted to call the meeting himself. So I made posting something any student can do: an event shows up right away and expires on its own when the date passes. And a profile leads with which days you’re on campus, because your schedule is what decides whether two commuters can ever meet.

What I would do next time

Take it back to the people I interviewed and watch them use it. The whole idea is that they have no time, and honestly I never measured whether CommU costs them any.

04

What changed

  • A feed to read→An event anyone can postI changed this because one student told me he wanted to set up the meetup himself, not just read about other people’s
  • Year and interests→Which days you are on campusI put this first on a profile because your schedule decides whether two commuters can ever meet. Year and interests don’t
  • Somebody maintaining a list→It expires on its ownAn event disappears once its date passes. I did it this way because nobody is going to clean up a list, and old events make the whole thing feel dead
  • 19 questions→3 findingsI boiled it down to three because I only wanted to build what students actually asked for, and each one became a part of the app
