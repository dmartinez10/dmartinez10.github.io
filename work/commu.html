Course project, University of Iowa, 2026

CommU

A web app that helps students who commute find out what’s happening on campus, and find each other. I ran the research, designed it and built it, front end and back end.

What CommU is

Students who commute come to campus for class and leave again. CommU puts campus events in one place, lets commuters find each other by the days they’re on campus, and lets any student post a meetup of their own.

The course asked for a working web app, not a clickable mockup. A mockup can show an idea, but only a working app shows whether the idea holds up.

Why this project

It was my project for Human-Computer Interaction, CS 2520. I chose commuters because most campus tools are built around students who live in the dorms, and commuters often struggle to feel like they belong.

What was mine

All of it, in one semester. I recruited and ran the five interviews, analysed them, designed the app, and built the front end and the back end.

  • 5interviews, across three schools
  • 19questions in the interview guide

01

What I did

  • 5interviews: 4 commuters, 1 on campus

    Recruited and ran the interviews

    I talked with four commuters and one student who lives on campus. I included the on-campus student on purpose: hearing from someone outside the group I was designing for showed me which problems were truly about commuting.

  • 19 → 3questions to findings

    Turned the interviews into three findings

    A 19-question guide, a list of topics rather than a fixed script, produced three findings. I built nothing a finding didn’t ask for, so every feature traces back to something a student said.

  • 8pages, front end and back end

    Designed and built the whole app

    Eight pages in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with a Flask and MongoDB backend, the server and database that store events and profiles. The course required a working app, so I built both ends myself.

02

Try it: what they said

Show a finding

I usually just show up a couple minutes before class and after school I have to go to work, which is about 30 minutes away. So, I usually don’t spend much time on campus.

P2, community college, one hour commute

Time on campus is only for class. It’s not that they don’t care. There’s just no room in the day for anything else.

Sometimes I get sent an email about upcoming events, other times it’s word of mouth through friends, and posters that are around buildings.

P4, engineering, walks in

Nobody knew what was going on. So the app puts every event in one place, with search and filters.

Yes, I would love to connect with people. I love meeting new people, but I just don’t put myself out there. If there’s an easier way, then yeah.

P5, allied health, 30 minute drive

The barrier was never motivation. There was just no place to meet, so the app gives them one.

03

The answer that changed the build

I first pitched CommU as a place to find events. Then I asked one student what he would change, and he said this:

It would be to host some sort of event to meet other people, because there have been people that I’ve met that live in my same complex and I had no idea.

P4, on what he would change

He didn’t want a better list of events. He wanted to start the meetup himself. That changed the app from something students read into something they use, and the decisions below follow from it.

What I would do next time

Take it back to the people I interviewed and watch them use it. The whole premise is that they have no spare time, and I haven’t yet measured how much of it CommU takes.

04

How I thought it through

  1. Students wanted to make plans, not just read about them

    What I saw
    Asked what he would change, one student (P4) said he wanted a way to host an event, because he had neighbours in his own complex he never knew about.
    What I decided
    Let any student post an event, and show it right away with no approval step.
    Why
    An events feed only helps people who already have time to browse. Posting lets a commuter create the moment that fits their own schedule.
    Result
    A feed to readAn event anyone can postThe third card on the home page, Post Your Own.

  2. Two commuters can only meet if they’re on campus the same day

    What I saw
    Commuters arrive for class and leave right after. Shared interests mean little if your days never overlap.
    What I decided
    Lead every profile with which days you’re on campus, then major and where you commute from.
    Why
    Schedule is the deciding factor, so it should be the first thing you see when choosing who to reach out to.
    Result
    Year and interests firstCampus days firstYou can see at a glance who you could actually meet this week.

  3. A list nobody maintains goes stale fast

    What I saw
    Events come from students, and nobody is assigned to clean up old ones.
    What I decided
    Every event removes itself once its date passes.
    Why
    Past events crowd out real ones and make the app look abandoned. Expiry keeps it accurate with no one tending it.
    Result
    Somebody maintaining a listIt expires on its ownEverything on the events page is something you can still go to.

  4. Five interviews give a lot of answers; the app needed a focus

    What I saw
    Five interviews on a 19-question guide produced far more ideas than one semester could build.
    What I decided
    Group what students said into three findings, and build only what a finding called for.
    Why
    Every feature should answer a real problem a student described. Anything else adds weight without helping.
    Result
    19 questions3 findingsEach one became a card on the home page: Event Discovery, Commuter Connect, Post Your Own.
