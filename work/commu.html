Course project, University of Iowa, 2026

CommU

I turned five interviews with commuting students into a working web app that helps them find campus events and each other, front end and back end.

Students who commute come to campus for class and leave again. Most campus tools are built around students who live in the dorms, so commuters miss what’s happening and struggle to feel like they belong.

01

Before and after

Before

Events reached commuters through scattered emails, word of mouth and posters on buildings. There was no way to find other commuters on campus the same days, and no easy place to meet.

After

Every campus event sits in one place, with search and filters. Commuters find each other by the days they’re on campus, and any student can post a meetup of their own. Past events remove themselves, so the list stays current.

02

How I worked

  1. Research

    Five interviews, three schools

    I recruited four commuters and one student who lives on campus, using a 19-question guide, a list of topics rather than a fixed script. The on-campus student showed me which problems were really about commuting.

  2. Define

    Three findings, nothing extra

    Time on campus is only for class. Nobody knows what’s going on, because the information is scattered. And students want to connect but have nowhere to do it. I built nothing a finding didn’t ask for.

  3. Design

    Profiles lead with campus days

    A profile shows which days you’re on campus, your major and where you commute from. Those three things decide whether two people can actually meet, so they come first.

  4. Build

    Eight working pages, both ends

    I built it in HTML, CSS and JavaScript with a Flask and MongoDB backend, the server and database that store events and profiles. Each event removes itself once its date passes, so nobody has to clean up the list.

  5. Test

    What I’d test next

    I haven’t put it in front of the students yet. Next I’d take it back to the people I interviewed and watch them use it, because the whole premise is that they have no spare time, and I haven’t measured how much of it CommU takes.

03

The turning point

The fork in the road

I first pitched CommU as a place to find events. Then one student told me he wanted to host one himself.

Why I went this way

He didn’t want a better list. He had neighbours in his own complex he’d never met. A feed only helps people with time to browse; posting lets a commuter create a moment that fits their own schedule. The easier path was a polished events list. I added Post Your Own instead, with no approval step.

04

The proof

  • 5students interviewed, at three schools
  • 19questions in the guide, topics not a script
  • 3findings, each one a card on the home page
  • 8working pages, front end and back end
